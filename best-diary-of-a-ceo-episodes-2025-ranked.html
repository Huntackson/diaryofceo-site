Best Diary of a CEO Episodes 2025: The Top 20 Ranked

Updated March 2025 • 12 min read • Diary of a CEO Fan Hub

2025 was a landmark year for The Diary of a CEO. Steven Bartlett's podcast cemented itself as the world's most influential long-form interview show, with episodes regularly surpassing 10 million views on YouTube and clips dominating every social platform imaginable.

But with hundreds of episodes now in the catalogue, which ones actually deserve your time? We've ranked the best Diary of a CEO episodes in 2025 based on viewer engagement, viral impact, and — most importantly — the density of life-changing insights packed into each conversation.

Whether you're a day-one listener or someone who just discovered the show, this is your definitive guide to the episodes that defined the year.

How We Ranked the Best DOAC Episodes of 2025

Our ranking methodology weighs three factors equally: YouTube engagement (views, likes, comment volume), social virality (how many clips from the episode went viral on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and X), and actionable value (did the episode give listeners something they could implement immediately?).

Episodes that simply entertained didn't rank as high as those that genuinely changed how people think, work, or live. For a broader look at the show, check out our complete guide to what the podcast is about.

The Top 20 Best Diary of a CEO Episodes in 2025

#1 — Alex Hormozi: The $100M Framework for Building Wealth

Hormozi's second appearance on the show was nothing short of electric. He broke down his "value equation" in granular detail — explaining exactly how to price products, create irresistible offers, and scale without burning out. The episode generated over 15 million YouTube views and spawned dozens of viral clips. His advice on "selling the vacation, not the plane ticket" became one of the most-quoted business phrases of the year.

#2 — Dr. Andrew Huberman: Rewire Your Brain in 30 Days

Huberman returned with a protocol-heavy episode that listeners could immediately apply. He covered dopamine management, the science of cold exposure, and a morning routine backed by peer-reviewed research. The "first 90 minutes" framework — where Huberman explains why the first 90 minutes after waking determine your entire day — became a cultural phenomenon.

#3 — Mel Robbins: The Let Them Theory

Robbins introduced a concept so simple it felt revolutionary: stop trying to control other people and focus exclusively on what you can control. "Let them" became a mantra for millions. The clip where she explains the theory surpassed 25 million views across platforms, making it the single most viral DOAC moment of 2025.

#4 — Dr. Paul Conti: The Hidden Force Controlling Your Life

This episode explored the unconscious mind, unresolved trauma, and why most people repeat destructive patterns without knowing it. Dr. Conti's explanation of the "structure and function of self" gave listeners a framework for genuine self-understanding. Steven Bartlett himself called this "the episode that changed me the most."

#5 — Chris Williamson: Why Loneliness Is Killing Us

Williamson and Bartlett tackled the modern loneliness epidemic with raw honesty. They discussed dating culture, social media's impact on real connection, and why men in particular are struggling to build meaningful friendships. The episode sparked a conversation that lasted weeks on social media. Read more in our relationship advice breakdown.

#6 — Simon Sinek: Why Empathy Is the Last Competitive Advantage

Sinek's thesis was bold: in a world where AI can do almost everything, the ability to genuinely understand and connect with other humans is the only skill that can't be automated. His leadership frameworks were practical, specific, and immediately applicable. For more on this episode, see our Simon Sinek deep dive.

#7 — Dr. Mindy Pelz: The Fasting Protocol That Changes Everything

Dr. Pelz delivered the most actionable health episode of the year, complete with specific fasting windows tailored by age, gender, and health goals. Her explanation of autophagy — the body's cellular cleaning process — made complex science accessible. Listeners reported measurable health improvements within weeks of following her protocols.

#8 — Gary Vaynerchuk: Attention Is the New Currency

Gary Vee brought his signature intensity to a conversation about why traditional marketing is dead and how creators are the new economy. His prediction that "every business will become a media company within 5 years" sparked heated debate. The episode is essential listening for anyone building a brand or business in 2025.

#9 — Gabor Maté: The Myth of Normal

Dr. Maté challenged everything we think we know about health, addiction, and what "normal" really means in modern society. His argument that most chronic illness stems from childhood stress was both disturbing and liberating. This episode doesn't just inform — it fundamentally shifts your worldview.

#10 — Sahil Bloom: Wealth Habits Nobody Teaches You

Bloom's episode was a masterclass in financial thinking. His "three bucket" investment framework, advice on thinking in decades, and debunking of budgeting myths made this the most practical money episode of the year. Visit our money advice collection for more financial insights from the show.

#11 — Steven Bartlett (Solo): What Losing Millions Taught Me

Bartlett's solo episodes are always fan favorites, and this one hit different. He opened up about specific investments that went wrong, the emotional spiral that followed, and the frameworks he now uses to make better decisions. The vulnerability was disarming and the lessons were universal.

#12 — Jay Shetty: The Sunday Reset That Changed My Life

Shetty shared a specific weekly routine for reflection, intention-setting, and stress management. The "Sunday Reset" concept went viral, with thousands of people sharing their own versions on social media. His meditation techniques were practical enough for complete beginners. Explore more in our Jay Shetty episode breakdown.

#13 — Dr. Tim Spector: The Truth About Ultra-Processed Food

Dr. Spector's episode was a wake-up call about what's actually in the food we eat. His research on the gut microbiome and its connection to mental health, energy, and disease prevention was eye-opening. The "30 plants per week" challenge he proposed became one of the biggest health trends of 2025.

#14 — Dr. Rangan Chatterjee: The 4-Pillar Health Framework

Chatterjee simplified health into four categories — movement, nutrition, sleep, and stress — and gave listeners a specific action plan for each. No supplements, no biohacking gadgets, just sustainable habits. The episode resonated because it made getting healthy feel achievable rather than overwhelming. Check out our complete health tips guide.

#15 — Jordan Peterson: Order, Chaos, and Finding Meaning

Peterson's episode was philosophical, challenging, and deeply personal. He discussed the relationship between responsibility and meaning, why young men are struggling, and how to find purpose in an increasingly chaotic world. Love him or not, the conversation was one of the most intellectually stimulating of the year.

#16 — Brené Brown: The Power of Vulnerability in Business

Brown made a compelling case that vulnerability isn't weakness — it's the birthplace of innovation, creativity, and trust. Her distinction between "armored leadership" and "daring leadership" gave executives a new vocabulary for building better teams and cultures.

#17 — James Clear: Atomic Habits — What I Got Wrong

Clear returned to update his famous frameworks, admitting what he'd gotten wrong and sharing new research on habit formation. His concept of "habit stacking 2.0" — connecting new behaviors to emotional states rather than existing habits — was a genuine evolution of his earlier work.

#18 — Dr. Julie Smith: Why You Feel Anxious All the Time

Dr. Smith broke down the neuroscience of anxiety in a way that was both validating and empowering. Her "anxiety audit" technique — a five-minute exercise for identifying what's actually causing your anxiety — became one of the most shared mental health tools of the year. More mental health insights in our mental health episodes guide.

#19 — Tony Robbins: Energy Is Everything

Robbins brought his legendary energy to a conversation about peak performance, financial freedom, and the morning rituals that fuel the world's most successful people. His "priming" exercise — a 10-minute morning routine combining breathwork, gratitude, and visualization — gave listeners an immediate action step.

#20 — Marisa Peer: I Am Enough — Rewiring Your Self-Worth

Peer's episode tackled the root cause of most human suffering: the belief that we're not enough. Her rapid transformational therapy techniques were demonstrated live on the podcast, and the results were emotional and powerful. This episode has been credited by thousands of listeners as the one that genuinely changed their self-perception.

Common Themes Across the Best Episodes of 2025

Looking at the top 20, several patterns emerge that tell us what listeners cared about most this year:

Mental health came first. Episodes about anxiety, trauma, and emotional wellbeing consistently outperformed pure business content. The stigma around mental health discussions has evaporated, and listeners are hungry for practical tools.

Protocols beat philosophy. The highest-ranked episodes gave listeners specific, step-by-step frameworks they could implement immediately. Vague inspiration doesn't cut it anymore — people want actionable systems.

Vulnerability drives virality. The episodes that went most viral were the ones where guests (or Bartlett himself) opened up about failure, fear, and doubt. Authenticity isn't just a buzzword — it's the currency of modern media.

"The best conversations aren't the ones where someone tells you what to think. They're the ones that make you question everything you thought you knew." — Steven Bartlett

How to Get the Most From These Episodes

Don't try to binge all 20. Pick the three that speak to your biggest current challenge — whether that's health, money, relationships, or mindset — and listen deeply. Take notes. Implement one takeaway before moving on to the next episode.
