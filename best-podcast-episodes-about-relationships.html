Best Diary of a CEO Episodes About Relationships

The most powerful conversations about love, attachment, dating, and building deeper human connections — from world-class therapists and relationship experts.

Some of the most-watched episodes of The Diary of a CEO aren't about business at all — they're about relationships. Steven Bartlett has brought on leading therapists, psychologists, and relationship experts who've helped millions understand why they love the way they do.

If you're searching for the best podcast episodes about relationships, this list features conversations that listeners call "life-changing." Here are the episodes you need to hear.

1. Matthew Hussey — "The Truth About Modern Dating"

Dating Self-Worth

Matthew Hussey, one of the world's most followed dating coaches, delivers a brutally honest conversation about why smart, attractive people struggle in dating. He explains the "investment gap" — why people chase those who show the least interest.

"You don't need to be chosen. You need to be someone who chooses wisely. The shift from 'pick me' to 'let me evaluate you' changes everything about your dating life."— Matthew Hussey

This episode racked up tens of millions of views for a reason. Hussey's advice is practical, direct, and immediately applicable.

2. Dr. Gabor Maté — "The Root of All Human Suffering"

Attachment Trauma

Dr. Gabor Maté connects childhood attachment patterns to adult relationship struggles. He explains why people repeat toxic relationship patterns and how unresolved trauma shows up in every romantic partnership.

"We don't get addicted to substances or people. We get addicted to the relief they provide from the pain we've been carrying since childhood."— Dr. Gabor Maté

This is arguably the most emotionally impactful episode in the entire DOAC catalogue. If you've ever wondered why you're attracted to the "wrong" people, Maté explains the neuroscience behind it.

3. Esther Perel — "Why People Cheat and How to Prevent It"

Infidelity Intimacy

Esther Perel, the world's foremost expert on modern relationships, challenges everything you think you know about infidelity. She argues that affairs aren't always about the partner — they're often about the person seeking a different version of themselves.

"The quality of your relationships determines the quality of your life. Not your career, not your bank account — your relationships."— Esther Perel

Perel's nuanced, non-judgmental approach to the hardest relationship topics makes this episode essential listening for anyone in a long-term partnership.

4. Dr. Julie Smith — "Why You Feel Lost and How to Find Yourself"

Mental Health Self-Discovery

Dr. Julie Smith, clinical psychologist and author of Why Has Nobody Told Me This Before?, explores how your relationship with yourself directly impacts every other relationship in your life. She provides actionable tools for building emotional resilience.

"You cannot pour from an empty cup, but you also can't fill your cup if you don't know what drains it. Self-awareness is the starting point of every healthy relationship."— Dr. Julie Smith

5. Derren Brown — "The Secret to Reading People"

Communication Psychology

Derren Brown brings a unique lens to relationships — the ability to truly read people. He discusses how most miscommunication comes not from what people say, but from the stories we tell ourselves about what they mean.

"We walk around thinking we see the world as it is. We don't. We see the world as we are. And that projection affects every relationship we have."— Derren Brown

Common Threads Across These Episodes

Your childhood shapes your love life. Dr. Gabor Maté and Dr. Julie Smith both emphasize that attachment patterns formed in childhood replay throughout adult relationships — until you become conscious of them.

Self-worth is the foundation. Matthew Hussey and Esther Perel agree: the relationship you have with yourself sets the ceiling for every other relationship. You can't receive love you don't believe you deserve.

Communication is a skill, not a talent. Every guest on this list treats communication as something you learn and practice, not something you're born with. Better conversations lead to better relationships.

Who Should Listen to These Episodes

These episodes are for anyone who wants to understand relationships at a deeper level — whether you're single and dating, in a long-term partnership, or recovering from a breakup. The insights apply to romantic relationships, friendships, and family dynamics.

Each conversation runs about 1.5 hours, but the frameworks you'll take away can reshape how you connect with people for the rest of your life.
