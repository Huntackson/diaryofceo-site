Business and Entrepreneurship Books

The business books recommended on DOAC aren't your typical airport reads. These are the books that the guests themselves — many of them worth hundreds of millions — say fundamentally shaped how they think about building companies.

$100M Offers by Alex Hormozi

Recommended by: Alex Hormozi (multiple episodes) & Steven Bartlett

Hormozi's own book came up repeatedly across his DOAC appearances. The core premise: most businesses fail not because of bad products, but because of bad offers. The book provides a framework for creating offers so good that people feel stupid saying no. Steven called it "the most practical business book I've ever read."

"Every business problem is an offer problem in disguise. Fix the offer, and 80% of your other problems disappear." — Alex Hormozi on The Diary of a CEO

The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel

Recommended by: Morgan Housel, Steven Bartlett, multiple guests

This was arguably the most-recommended book across all DOAC episodes. Housel's central argument — that financial success is more about behavior than knowledge — resonated with nearly every guest who discussed money. Steven has called it his "desert island book" for financial literacy.

"The premise of this book is that doing well with money has a little to do with how smart you are and a lot to do with how you behave. And behavior is hard to teach, even to really smart people." — Morgan Housel on The Diary of a CEO

Zero to One by Peter Thiel

Recommended by: Steven Bartlett, Alex Hormozi

Thiel's contrarian take on startups — that the best businesses create something entirely new rather than competing in existing markets — was referenced multiple times on DOAC. Steven specifically highlighted the "what important truth do very few people agree with you on?" framework as one of the most powerful questions in business.

Good to Great by Jim Collins

Recommended by: Multiple guests across business episodes

Collins' research-backed study of what separates good companies from great ones was mentioned in at least a dozen DOAC episodes. The "Hedgehog Concept" — finding the intersection of what you're deeply passionate about, what you can be the best in the world at, and what drives your economic engine — became a recurring framework on the show.

The Lean Startup by Eric Ries

Recommended by: Steven Bartlett

Steven has cited this as one of the books that shaped his early career at Social Chain. The build-measure-learn feedback loop and the concept of the "minimum viable product" came up naturally in many entrepreneurship-focused episodes.

Psychology and Self-Understanding Books

The psychology section of Diary of a CEO book recommendations is exceptionally strong, thanks to the caliber of mental health professionals and researchers who appear on the show.

The Body Keeps the Score by Bessel van der Kolk

Recommended by: Dr. Gabor Maté, Steven Bartlett, multiple guests

This was the most-recommended psychology book across all DOAC episodes. Van der Kolk's groundbreaking work on how trauma lives in the body (not just the mind) was referenced by nearly every mental health guest. Dr. Gabor Maté called it "the book that changed how the world understands trauma."

"You can't talk your way out of trauma that's stored in your body. This book explains why, and more importantly, what to do about it." — Dr. Gabor Maté on The Diary of a CEO

Thinking, Fast and Slow by Daniel Kahneman

Recommended by: Steven Bartlett, Robert Greene, multiple guests

Kahneman's masterwork on the two systems of human thought — the fast, intuitive system and the slow, deliberate system — came up in conversations about decision-making, cognitive bias, and why smart people make dumb choices. Robert Greene specifically cited it as essential reading for understanding human nature.

When the Body Says No by Gabor Maté

Recommended by: Dr. Gabor Maté (his own book), referenced by Steven Bartlett

Maté's exploration of the mind-body connection in illness was a centerpiece of his DOAC episode. The book argues that chronic stress and emotional repression manifest as physical disease — a claim that was controversial when published but is now supported by extensive research.

Attached by Amir Levine and Rachel Heller

Recommended by: Steven Bartlett, relationship-focused episodes

This book on attachment theory in adult relationships was discussed extensively in DOAC episodes about dating and love. Understanding whether you're secure, anxious, or avoidant in relationships was described as "the single most useful framework for understanding why your relationships keep failing."

Man's Search for Meaning by Viktor Frankl

Recommended by: Jordan Peterson, Steven Bartlett, Jay Shetty

Frankl's account of surviving the Holocaust and finding meaning even in the most extreme suffering was cited by multiple guests as the most important book they've ever read. Jordan Peterson called it "the book that proves that meaning can sustain you through anything — literally anything."

"Those who have a 'why' to live, can bear with almost any 'how.' That's not a metaphor. Frankl proved it in the most extreme conditions imaginable." — Jordan Peterson on The Diary of a CEO

Health and Longevity Books

The health episodes on DOAC feature world-leading scientists and doctors, and their book recommendations reflect the cutting edge of wellness research.

Why We Sleep by Matthew Walker

Recommended by: Dr. Matthew Walker (his own book), universally referenced

Possibly the most impactful health book discussed on DOAC. Walker's argument that sleep is the foundation of all other health behaviors — and that we're in the midst of a "sleep loss epidemic" — has been echoed by virtually every health guest on the show.

"This is the book that made me take sleep seriously. I used to brag about sleeping five hours. Now I protect my eight hours like my life depends on it — because it does." — Steven Bartlett, The Diary of a CEO

Outlive by Peter Attia

Recommended by: Multiple health-focused guests

Attia's comprehensive guide to longevity was a frequent recommendation in 2024 DOAC episodes. The book's framework for thinking about healthspan (not just lifespan) and its evidence-based approach to exercise, nutrition, sleep, and emotional health made it a natural fit for the show's health discussions.

The 4-Hour Body by Tim Ferriss

Recommended by: Steven Bartlett

Ferriss' experimental approach to physical optimization was cited as an early influence on Steven's interest in health science. While some of the specific protocols have been updated by newer research, the book's core philosophy — test everything, measure the results, keep what works — remains a throughline in DOAC health episodes.

Breath by James Nestor

Recommended by: Referenced in multiple health episodes

Nestor's investigation into the lost art of breathing was discussed across several DOAC episodes, particularly those featuring Dr. Andrew Huberman. The book's argument that most modern humans breathe incorrectly — and that fixing this single habit can transform health — aligned perfectly with the show's emphasis on foundational health practices.

Mindset and Philosophy Books

These Diary of a CEO book recommendations tend to be the ones that guests describe as "life-changing" rather than just informative.

Meditations by Marcus Aurelius

Recommended by: Ryan Holiday (referenced), Steven Bartlett, multiple guests

The ancient Stoic text was the most-referenced philosophy book across all DOAC episodes. Multiple guests cited specific passages about controlling your response to events, accepting mortality, and focusing on what's within your control. Steven has described it as "the original self-help book."

The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck by Mark Manson

Recommended by: Mark Manson (his own book), referenced across episodes

Manson's counterintuitive approach to happiness — that we need to choose our struggles more carefully rather than trying to eliminate them — became a central topic during his DOAC episode. The book's practical framework for deciding what actually deserves your emotional energy resonated deeply with listeners.

Atomic Habits by James Clear

Recommended by: Multiple guests, Steven Bartlett

Clear's system for building good habits and breaking bad ones was referenced in dozens of DOAC episodes. The core concept — that tiny improvements compound into remarkable results — became almost a default framework on the show. It was particularly prominent in episodes featuring Dr. Andrew Huberman, who praised its alignment with neuroscience research.

"You don't rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems. That line from Atomic Habits changed how I think about everything." — Steven Bartlett, The Diary of a CEO

Think Like a Monk by Jay Shetty

Recommended by: Jay Shetty (his own book)

Shetty's synthesis of ancient monastic wisdom and modern psychology was the foundation of his DOAC episode. The book's practical exercises for building morning routines, managing digital distractions, and cultivating inner peace were described as "Stoicism for the smartphone generation."

The Laws of Human Nature by Robert Greene

Recommended by: Robert Greene (his own book), Steven Bartlett

Greene's comprehensive guide to understanding human behavior was a centerpiece of his DOAC episode. Steven called it "the book that taught me more about people than any other." The sections on recognizing narcissism, understanding envy, and developing empathy were particularly highlighted.

Steven Bartlett's Personal Top 5

Across multiple episodes and interviews, Steven has narrowed his recommendations to these five books he believes everyone should read:

  1. The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel — "Changed my relationship with money forever"
  2. The Body Keeps the Score by Bessel van der Kolk — "Made me understand my own patterns"
  3. Atomic Habits by James Clear — "The most practical self-improvement book ever written"
  4. $100M Offers by Alex Hormozi — "The most practical business book I've ever read"
  5. Man's Search for Meaning by Viktor Frankl — "The book that puts everything in perspective"

How to Actually Read These Books

A reading list is only useful if you actually read the books. Here's a practical approach to getting through the Diary of a CEO book recommendations:

  • Start with one. Don't try to read five books at once. Pick the one that addresses your biggest current challenge and commit to it.
  • Listen to the DOAC episode first. Hearing the author or recommender discuss the book gives you context that makes the reading experience richer. Get episode summaries at diaryofceo.online.
  • Use audiobooks. Many of these books have excellent audio versions. Listen during commutes, workouts, or walks.
  • Take notes. The books that change your life are the ones you engage with actively. Highlight, annotate, and write down the ideas that challenge you.
  • Discuss with others. The most powerful insights come from discussing ideas, not just consuming them. Share the book with a friend and compare notes.

The Diary of a CEO book recommendations span decades of accumulated wisdom from some of the world's most accomplished people. Whether you read one or all of them, each book on this list has the potential to shift your perspective in ways that ripple through every area of your life.
