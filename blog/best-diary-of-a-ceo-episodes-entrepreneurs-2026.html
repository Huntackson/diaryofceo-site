Best Diary of a CEO Episodes for Entrepreneurs 2026 — Top 15 Ranked

The Diary of a CEO has quietly become the most valuable business education on the internet — and it's completely free. Steven Bartlett has interviewed some of the most successful entrepreneurs, investors, and thinkers alive, and the lessons packed into these 1.5-hour conversations would cost tens of thousands of dollars at any business school.

But with hundreds of episodes in the archive, where do you start? We've ranked the 15 best Diary of a CEO episodes for entrepreneurs in 2026 based on three criteria:

  1. Actionability — Can you apply the advice immediately?
  2. Depth — Does the guest go beyond surface-level platitudes?
  3. Results — Have listeners and viewers reported real-world results from following the advice?

Whether you're launching your first side hustle or scaling a seven-figure business, these episodes will give you frameworks, strategies, and mindset shifts that actually move the needle.

For full summaries of each episode, visit diaryofceo.online.

#1: Alex Hormozi — "The Man That Makes Millionaires"

Why It's #1: No episode in DOAC history has generated more direct business results. Alex Hormozi breaks down his $100M Offers framework — a step-by-step system for creating offers so good that customers feel stupid saying no.

Hormozi doesn't deal in theory. He gives you the exact equation:

"Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice). If you can maximize the top and minimize the bottom, you have an offer people can't refuse." — Alex Hormozi, Founder of Acquisition.com, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • How to price your product based on the value it delivers, not the cost to produce
  • Why "free" is often more expensive than premium pricing
  • The exact script Hormozi used to go from $1,000 to $100 million
  • Why you should solve the customer's next problem inside the current product
Best For: Anyone selling a product or service who wants to dramatically increase revenue without increasing traffic.

Read our full Alex Hormozi DOAC summary.

#2: Naval Ravikant — Wealth, Happiness & Leverage

Why It's Ranked Here: Naval's episode is the most intellectually rich conversation Steven Bartlett has ever hosted. Naval doesn't give you tactics — he gives you thinking frameworks that compound for the rest of your life.

"Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep." — Naval Ravikant, Investor & Philosopher

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • The three types of leverage and why code/media are the most powerful
  • Why specific knowledge — the skills only you can develop — is your unfair advantage
  • How to make decisions faster using Naval's "hell yes or no" framework
  • Why reading foundational books matters more than business books
Best For: Entrepreneurs who want to build long-term, sustainable wealth rather than just short-term revenue.

#3: Tony Robbins — Mindset, Money & Mastering Life

Why It's Ranked Here: If Hormozi gives you the business strategy and Naval gives you the philosophy, Tony Robbins gives you the emotional and psychological tools to execute. His episode is a masterclass in state management, identity, and peak performance.

"The strongest force in the human personality is the need to stay consistent with how we define ourselves. Change your identity, change your life." — Tony Robbins, Life Coach & Author

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • How to use the 10-minute morning priming routine to start every day in peak state
  • Why your identity — not your skills — determines your income ceiling
  • The six human needs framework for understanding customer behaviour
  • How to model successful people instead of reinventing the wheel
Best For: Entrepreneurs who know what to do but struggle with consistency, motivation, or self-sabotage.

Read our full Tony Robbins DOAC summary.

#4: Simon Sinek — Start With Why

Why It's Ranked Here: Simon Sinek's episode is essential for any entrepreneur who wants to build a brand that lasts. He explains the Golden Circle framework — why the most successful companies (Apple, Patagonia, Southwest Airlines) start with WHY they exist, not WHAT they sell.

"People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it. And what you do simply proves what you believe." — Simon Sinek, Author & Leadership Expert, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • How to define your "why" so it resonates with customers on an emotional level
  • Why infinite-minded companies outlast finite-minded competitors
  • The trust equation: consistency + authenticity over time = unshakeable loyalty
  • How to lead a team by inspiring them rather than managing them
Best For: Founders building a brand, leaders managing teams, or anyone who feels disconnected from the purpose behind their work.

#5: Gary Vaynerchuk — The Social Media Playbook

Why It's Ranked Here: Gary Vee's DOAC episode is the most practical social media marketing masterclass available for free. He breaks down exactly how to build an audience, create content at scale, and monetize attention — all without spending money on ads.

"Attention is the most valuable asset in the world right now. If you can capture and hold attention, you can sell anything." — Gary Vaynerchuk, CEO of VaynerMedia, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • The $1.80 strategy for building genuine engagement on any platform
  • Why you should produce 100 pieces of content per day (and how that's actually feasible)
  • How to use TikTok and Instagram Reels as a free customer acquisition machine
  • Why patience is the most underrated business strategy
Best For: Entrepreneurs who need to build an audience and generate leads without a big marketing budget.

#6: James Clear — Atomic Habits for Business

Why It's Ranked Here: James Clear's episode translates the Atomic Habits framework specifically for business outcomes. If you've read the book, this conversation goes deeper — with specific applications for entrepreneurs who struggle with consistency.

"You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems." — James Clear, Author of Atomic Habits, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • How to design your environment so good habits become automatic
  • The two-minute rule for overcoming procrastination on important business tasks
  • Why identity-based habits outperform outcome-based goals
  • How to stack new habits onto existing routines for effortless consistency
Best For: Entrepreneurs who know what they should be doing but can't stay consistent.

#7: Mel Robbins — The 5 Second Rule & The Let Them Theory

Why It's Ranked Here: Mel Robbins' episode is the most emotionally impactful business conversation on DOAC. She combines neuroscience with raw honesty to explain why we self-sabotage — and exactly how to stop.

"If you have an instinct to act on a goal, you must physically move within 5 seconds or your brain will kill the idea." — Mel Robbins, Author & Motivational Speaker, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • The 5-second rule for overcoming hesitation and taking immediate action
  • The "Let Them" theory: stop trying to control what others think and focus on what you can control
  • How high-functioning anxiety disguises itself as productivity
  • Why the stories you tell yourself about failure are more damaging than failure itself
Best For: Entrepreneurs dealing with imposter syndrome, overthinking, or fear of failure.

#8: Jordan Peterson — Discipline, Meaning & Responsibility

Why It's Ranked Here: Jordan Peterson's DOAC episodes are among the most viewed in the podcast's history. His perspective on discipline, responsibility, and finding meaning through work is powerful for entrepreneurs who need intellectual depth behind their ambition.

"Compare yourself to who you were yesterday, not to who someone else is today." — Jordan Peterson, Psychologist & Author, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • Why pursuing meaning is more sustainable than pursuing happiness
  • How to use responsibility — not motivation — as the engine for daily execution
  • The relationship between order and chaos in creative work
  • Why telling the truth (especially to yourself) is the foundation of trust
Best For: Entrepreneurs seeking deeper philosophical grounding for their work and decisions.

#9: Andrew Huberman — Neuroscience of Focus & Performance

Why It's Ranked Here: Dr. Huberman's episode is the most science-backed performance guide on DOAC. He explains exactly how your brain works — and how to optimise it for focus, energy, and decision-making.

"Your first morning sunlight exposure sets your entire circadian rhythm for the day. Get 10 minutes of natural light within 30 minutes of waking and your focus, energy, and sleep will all improve." — Dr. Andrew Huberman, Neuroscientist, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • The science behind optimal work schedules (90-minute deep work blocks)
  • How dopamine management affects motivation and why social media destroys it
  • Cold exposure and its evidence-based effects on focus and energy
  • How to design a sleep protocol that gives you an extra 2-3 hours of productive time
Best For: Entrepreneurs who want to optimise their biology for peak performance.

#10: Robert Greene — The 48 Laws of Power Applied to Business

Why It's Ranked Here: Robert Greene's episode is a strategic masterclass. He applies the principles from his bestselling books — The 48 Laws of Power, Mastery, and The Laws of Human Nature — directly to business and entrepreneurship.

"The key to power is the ability to judge who is best able to further your interests in all situations." — Robert Greene, Author, on Diary of a CEO

Key Lessons for Entrepreneurs:
  • How to read people and understand their true motivations in negotiations
  • The mastery process: 10,000 hours of deliberate practice with a mentor
  • Why emotional intelligence matters more than IQ in business
  • How to protect yourself from manipulative partners, investors, and competitors
Best For: Entrepreneurs navigating complex business relationships, partnerships, or negotiations.

#11: Brené Brown — Vulnerability as a Business Superpower

Brené Brown's conversation with Steven Bartlett challenged the "tough founder" stereotype. She argued that vulnerability isn't weakness — it's the birthplace of innovation, creativity, and trust.

"Vulnerability is not winning or losing. It's having the courage to show up when you can't control the outcome." — Brené Brown, Researcher & Author, on Diary of a CEO

Best For: Founders who struggle with asking for help, admitting mistakes, or building genuine team culture.

#12: Tim Ferriss — The 4-Hour Entrepreneur

Tim Ferriss brought his signature approach of deconstructing success into specific, replicable steps. His episode focused on lifestyle design, 80/20 analysis, and fear-setting — a framework he argues is more valuable than goal-setting.

Best For: Entrepreneurs who feel overwhelmed and need to ruthlessly simplify their focus.

#13: Mo Gawdat — The Happiness Equation for Founders

Mo Gawdat, former Chief Business Officer at Google X, brought a mathematician's mind to the question of happiness. His equation — Happiness ≥ Events - Expectations — is brutally simple and incredibly effective.

Best For: Entrepreneurs who've achieved external success but feel empty or burned out.

#14: Dr. Rangan Chatterjee — Health as a Business Strategy

Dr. Chatterjee's episode reframed health not as self-care but as a competitive advantage. He argued that entrepreneurs who optimise their sleep, nutrition, and movement will outperform competitors who rely on caffeine and willpower.

Best For: Entrepreneurs who sacrifice health for hustle and are starting to feel the consequences.

#15: Marisa Peer — Rewiring Your Money Mindset

Marisa Peer's episode is the sleeper hit of DOAC for entrepreneurs. She explained how subconscious beliefs about money — often formed in childhood — create invisible ceilings on your earning potential.

"You can have everything in life you want, if you will just help enough other people get what they want. But first, you have to believe you're enough." — Marisa Peer, Therapist & Author, on Diary of a CEO

Best For: Entrepreneurs who consistently plateau at the same income level or self-sabotage when things start going well.

Don't try to binge all 15 episodes. Instead, identify where you are right now:
