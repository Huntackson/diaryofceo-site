Tony Robbins Best Advice from Diary of a CEO: 10 Life-Changing Lessons

Tony Robbins' episode on The Diary of a CEO wasn't just a conversation � it was a 1.5-hour masterclass on how to redesign your entire life. From the opening question to the final thought, Robbins delivered the kind of advice that makes you pause the podcast, grab a notebook, and start writing.

This man has coached presidents, billionaires, and professional athletes. He's spoken to over 50 million people across four decades. And when he sat down with Steven Bartlett, he didn't hold back.

If you're looking for motivation that actually translates into action, this is the episode. Below are the 10 best pieces of advice Tony Robbins shared on Diary of a CEO � broken down, explained, and ready for you to apply today.

1. Change the Meaning, Change Your Life

The single most powerful idea Tony Robbins shared isn't about money, habits, or goals. It's about meaning. He explained to Steven that two people can go through the exact same experience � a bankruptcy, a breakup, a childhood trauma � and come out on the other side as completely different people. One becomes bitter. The other becomes unstoppable.

The difference? The story they tell themselves about what happened.

"It's not the events of our lives that shape us, but our beliefs as to what those events mean." � Tony Robbins, Life Coach & Bestselling Author, on Diary of a CEO

Tony used his own life as proof. Growing up with an abusive, alcoholic mother in poverty, he had every reason to become a statistic. Instead, he chose to make his pain mean something � it became the fuel that drove him to help millions of people transform their lives.

How to Apply This:

Write down the three most painful experiences of your life. Next to each one, write down the story you've been telling yourself about it. Now rewrite that story � not to deny the pain, but to find the lesson, the strength, or the redirection it gave you. This single exercise has changed more lives than any productivity hack ever will.

2. Your Identity Is Your Destiny

Tony told Steven something that stopped the conversation cold: most people fail not because they lack talent or opportunity, but because they have an identity that doesn't match their ambitions. If you see yourself as "someone who's bad with money," no financial advice in the world will save you. If your identity is "I'm not the kind of person who starts businesses," you won't start one.

"The strongest force in the human personality is the need to stay consistent with how we define ourselves." � Tony Robbins

He explained that identity operates like a thermostat. If your financial thermostat is set at $60,000 a year, you'll unconsciously sabotage any effort to earn significantly more � and if you dip below, you'll hustle until you're back at your set point.

How to Apply This:

Decide who you need to become before you decide what you need to do. Write an identity statement: "I am someone who ___." Make it specific. "I am someone who builds assets that generate income while I sleep." Then make every daily decision through the lens of that identity.

3. Master Your State Before You Master Anything Else

When Steven asked Tony about his morning routine, Robbins didn't talk about journaling or cold emails. He talked about state management � the art of controlling your emotional and physical state so you can perform at your peak regardless of external circumstances.

Tony's morning priming routine takes just 10 minutes:

  1. 3 minutes of rapid breathing � a specific pattern that floods the body with oxygen
  2. 3 minutes of gratitude � focusing on three things he's genuinely grateful for
  3. 3 minutes of visualisation � seeing himself achieving his most important goals
  4. 1 minute of intention � deciding the three outcomes that would make the day extraordinary

"If you don't have 10 minutes for yourself, you don't have a life." � Tony Robbins

How to Apply This:

Before checking your phone, email, or social media, spend 10 minutes on Tony's priming routine. The order matters: body first (breathing), then emotions (gratitude), then mind (visualisation and intention). Do this for 30 days and watch how differently you show up.

4. The Quality of Your Life Equals the Quality of Your Questions

Tony revealed to Steven that the questions you ask yourself on a daily basis literally determine the quality of your life. Most people ask disempowering questions: "Why does this always happen to me?" "What's wrong with me?" "Why can't I catch a break?"

These questions presuppose negative answers. Your brain will always find evidence to support whatever question you ask. Ask "Why am I unlucky?" and your brain will compile a highlight reel of every bad thing that's ever happened to you.

Tony flipped the script. He asks himself empowering questions every morning:

  • "What am I most grateful for right now?"
  • "What am I most excited about in my life?"
  • "What can I give today?"

"Quality questions create a quality life. Successful people ask better questions, and as a result, they get better answers." � Tony Robbins, on Diary of a CEO

How to Apply This:

Replace your default inner dialogue with three empowering questions. Write them on a sticky note next to your bed. Ask them before your feet hit the floor. This is not woo-woo � it's neuroscience. The reticular activating system in your brain prioritises information that matches your dominant focus.

5. Become an Owner, Not Just a Consumer

The money section of the episode was jaw-dropping. Tony distilled decades of financial wisdom � including insights from interviewing Warren Buffett, Ray Dalio, and Carl Icahn � into one brutally simple principle: stop being just a consumer and start being an owner.

Most people spend their entire working lives buying products from companies they never invest in. They drink Starbucks every day but never own a share of Starbucks stock. They use Apple products but never participate in Apple's growth.

Tony argued that the single biggest shift you can make is to divert a percentage of every pound or dollar you earn into assets � stocks, real estate, intellectual property, businesses � that generate returns while you sleep.

"The secret to wealth is simple: find a way to do more for others than anyone else does. Become more valuable. Then take a percentage of everything you earn and put it to work." � Tony Robbins

How to Apply This:

Set up an automatic transfer of at least 10% of your income into an investment account � before you see or spend the money. Increase it by 1% every quarter. Tony stressed that automation beats discipline because it removes the need for willpower entirely.

6. Model the Masters � Don't Reinvent the Wheel

Tony was blunt about this: original thinking is overrated. The fastest path to any result is to find someone who has already achieved it and model their strategy. He told Steven that every breakthrough in his career came not from creative genius but from studying and replicating what already worked.

"Success leaves clues. If you want to be successful, find someone who has achieved the results you want and copy what they do." � Tony Robbins

He described how he learned to help people overcome phobias in 15 minutes by studying the work of therapists who had already solved the problem. He didn't invent a new method � he modelled the best existing one and made it more efficient.

How to Apply This:

Identify the one outcome you want most right now. Find three people who've already achieved it. Study everything they've shared � books, interviews, podcasts, social media. Extract the common principles. Then execute those principles with your own energy and context.

7. Trade Your Expectations for Appreciation

This was one of the most emotionally powerful moments in the episode. Tony told Steven that the antidote to suffering is appreciation. Not in a vague, gratitude-journal way � but as a daily practice of shifting from expectation to wonder.

He explained that most unhappiness comes from the gap between expectations and reality. When you expect life to go a certain way and it doesn't, you suffer. But when you trade expectations for appreciation � genuinely appreciating what you have right now � suffering evaporates.

"When you are grateful, fear disappears and abundance appears. Trade your expectations for appreciation and your whole world changes in an instant." � Tony Robbins, on Diary of a CEO

How to Apply This:

Every evening, write down three specific things from the day that you genuinely appreciate. Not generic things like "my health" � specific moments, interactions, or realisations. This rewires your brain's negativity bias over time.

8. The Two Master Skills: The Science of Achievement and the Art of Fulfilment

Tony made a distinction that stopped Steven in his tracks. He said most people are great at one of two skills but terrible at the other:

  1. The Science of Achievement � setting goals, creating plans, taking action, producing results
  2. The Art of Fulfilment � being present, feeling grateful, enjoying the journey

He pointed to examples of wildly successful people � billionaires, celebrities, world champions � who achieved everything and still felt empty. Achievement without fulfilment, Tony said, is the ultimate failure.

"Success without fulfilment is the ultimate failure. I know billionaires who are miserable. The question isn't 'How do I achieve more?' � it's 'How do I enjoy what I've already achieved while pursuing what I want next?'" � Tony Robbins

How to Apply This:

Rate yourself from 1-10 on both skills right now. If achievement is high but fulfilment is low, your priority isn't another goal � it's reconnecting with why you started. If fulfilment is high but achievement is low, you need more structure, accountability, and execution habits.

9. Your Peer Group Is Your Future

Tony and Steven had a candid exchange about the people you surround yourself with. Tony's advice was uncompromising: your peer group will either pull you up or drag you down. There is no neutral influence.

He shared that when he was starting out, he actively sought relationships with people who were 10 or 20 years ahead of where he wanted to be. Not to network or extract value � but to raise his own standards by proximity.

"You are the average of the five people you spend the most time with. If you don't like the average, change the five." � Tony Robbins

How to Apply This:

Audit your five closest relationships. Are they expanding your thinking or reinforcing your comfort zone? You don't need to cut people off � but you do need to deliberately add people who operate at the level you aspire to. Podcasts, masterminds, mentors, and events all count.

10. Massive Action Cures Everything

The final and perhaps most important piece of advice Tony left Steven with was about action. Not just any action � massive, imperfect, relentless action. Tony argued that the difference between people who succeed and people who don't is simple: successful people take more action, faster, and they course-correct along the way.

"The path to success is to take massive, determined action." � Tony Robbins

He told a story about a time early in his career when he was broke and terrified. Instead of waiting until he felt confident, he made 100 phone calls in a single day. Most said no. But a handful said yes � and those yeses changed his trajectory forever.

How to Apply This:

Whatever goal you're working toward right now, 10x your action for the next 7 days. If you were going to send 5 outreach emails, send 50. If you were going to publish one post, publish five. Volume creates momentum. Momentum creates confidence. Confidence creates results.
