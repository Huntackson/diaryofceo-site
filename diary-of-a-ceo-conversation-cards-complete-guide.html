How to Play The DOAC Conversation Cards

There are no rigid rules � that's part of the appeal. But here are the most popular ways people play:

The Classic Circle

  1. Sit in a group (2+ people)
  2. One person draws a card and reads it aloud
  3. Everyone takes turns answering
  4. The person who drew the card answers last
  5. Pass to the next person to draw

No phones. No judgement. Just honest answers.

Couples Edition

For couples, the cards work beautifully as a date night activity. Sit facing each other, take turns drawing, and answer honestly. Many couples report learning things about their partner they never knew, even after years together.

The Solo Version

Yes, you can play alone. Draw a card each morning and journal your answer. It becomes a powerful self-reflection practice � essentially like doing a daily Diary of a CEO-style interview with yourself.

The Dinner Party Game

Place cards in the centre of the dinner table. Between courses, someone draws a card and the whole table discusses. It transforms a regular dinner into an unforgettable evening.

The Work Team Version

Start meetings with one card. It builds psychological safety, helps team members understand each other, and sets a tone of openness. Select cards appropriate for the workplace � the sets include a good mix of professional and personal questions.

Tips for Getting the Most Out of Your Cards

  1. Create a safe space. Establish ground rules: no judgement, no interrupting, and what's shared stays in the room. Vulnerability requires safety.
  2. Put phones away. This is essential. The cards lose their magic if people are half-paying attention.
  3. Don't rush. Let people take time to think. The best answers come after a pause.
  4. Go first. If you want others to be vulnerable, model it yourself. Give a genuine, honest answer and others will follow.
  5. Skip cards that don't fit. Not every question works in every setting. It's fine to draw another.
  6. Follow up. The best conversations happen when you dig deeper into someone's answer. Ask "why?" and "tell me more."
  7. Make it a ritual. Weekly card sessions with your partner or friends create compounding intimacy over time.
