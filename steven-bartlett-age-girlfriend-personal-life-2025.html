Steven Bartlett: Age, Girlfriend, Family & Personal Life — The Full Story Behind The Diary of a CEO

As the host of The Diary of a CEO — one of the world's most-listened-to podcasts — and the youngest-ever Dragon on BBC's Dragons' Den, he's built an empire that spans media, investing, and personal development. But behind the brand, who is Steven Bartlett the person?

Here's everything we know about his personal life, background, relationships, and the experiences that shaped the man behind the microphone.

Early Life: From Botswana to Plymouth

Steven was born in Botswana to a Nigerian mother and a British father. His family moved to Plymouth, England when he was two years old. Growing up in a working-class household, he's spoken candidly on the podcast about the challenges of his childhood — including financial struggles, his parents' separation, and feeling like an outsider in a predominantly white city.

"I grew up poor. Not 'couldn't afford holidays' poor — I mean my mum was choosing between heating and food. That's a different kind of education. It teaches you what money actually means." — Steven Bartlett, The Diary of a CEO

He's described his mother as the single biggest influence on his life. A Nigerian immigrant who worked multiple jobs to support her children, she instilled a relentless work ethic that Steven credits as the foundation of everything he's built.

How Old Is Steven Bartlett?

Steven Bartlett is currently 32 years old (born August 26, 1992). He'll turn 33 in August 2025. What makes his age remarkable is the timeline of his achievements:

Age 18 — Dropped out of university after one lecture. Moved into a house with no furniture or heating.
Age 21 — Co-founded Social Chain, a social media marketing company, from his bedroom in Manchester.
Age 26 — Took Social Chain public on the stock exchange, valued at over $200M.
Age 27 — Stepped down as CEO. Launched The Diary of a CEO podcast.
Age 28 — Became the youngest-ever Dragon on BBC's Dragons' Den.
Age 30 — The Diary of a CEO became the UK's #1 podcast. Published his book "Happy Sexy Millionaire."
Age 32 — Podcast surpassed 1 billion total downloads. Named one of the most influential people in UK media.

At 32, Steven has accomplished what most entrepreneurs spend a lifetime chasing. And he's spoken openly about how this accelerated timeline affected his mental health — a topic he explores regularly with guests like Dr. Julie Smith and Dr. Paul Conti.

Steven Bartlett's Girlfriend & Relationships

Steven keeps his romantic life relatively private, but he hasn't been entirely silent about it. He's been open about the challenges of maintaining relationships while building companies and a media empire.

He's been linked to several high-profile relationships over the years, and has occasionally shared insights about his love life on social media and in podcast episodes. However, he generally draws a clear line between his public and private life when it comes to romantic partners.

"I used to think relationships were a distraction from success. I was wrong. The most successful people I've interviewed on this podcast are deeply loved by someone. That's not a coincidence." — Steven Bartlett

In multiple episodes, Steven has discussed what he's learned about love from his guests — including insights from Matthew Hussey on dating confidence and relationship experts across the show.

Key themes he's shared about relationships:

  • Vulnerability is strength. Influenced by Brené Brown's episode, he's talked about learning to be emotionally open with partners.
  • Attachment styles matter. He's referenced understanding his own attachment patterns through therapy.
  • Work-life balance is a myth. He prefers "work-life integration" — finding a partner who understands the mission.
  • Communication is a skill. Not something you're born with, but something you practice deliberately.

Family & Cultural Identity

Being biracial — half Nigerian, half British — has shaped Steven's worldview profoundly. He's spoken about feeling caught between two cultures, not fully belonging to either growing up in Plymouth.

His relationship with his mother remains central to his identity. In one of the podcast's most emotional moments, he described calling his mum after Social Chain's IPO and realizing that the money meant nothing compared to hearing her say she was proud.

"My mum cleaned toilets so I could have opportunities she never had. Every morning I wake up, that's my motivation. Not the cars, not the followers — her." — Steven Bartlett

He's also been candid about his complicated relationship with his father, and how Gabor Maté's insights on childhood trauma helped him understand patterns in his own life.

Mental Health & Personal Growth

One of the most refreshing things about Steven is his willingness to discuss his mental health struggles publicly. Despite the outward success, he's talked about:

  • Depression and loneliness during the Social Chain years
  • Imposter syndrome — even while sitting in the Dragons' Den chair
  • The pressure of being a "role model" at a young age
  • How therapy changed his approach to relationships and business
