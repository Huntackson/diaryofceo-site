Who Is Steven Bartlett?

Steven Bartlett is a British-Nigerian entrepreneur, investor, author, and podcast host. Born in Botswana and raised in Plymouth, England, he dropped out of Manchester Metropolitan University at 22 to build Social Chain — a social media marketing company he grew to a £200M+ valuation and took public on the London Stock Exchange before age 30.

In 2022, he became the youngest-ever investor on BBC's Dragons' Den (the UK equivalent of Shark Tank), where he's invested in dozens of businesses. His book Happy Sexy Millionaire became a Sunday Times bestseller, challenging conventional wisdom about what success actually looks like.

But it's The Diary of a CEO that has made him a global figure. What started as a small passion project has become one of the world's most influential podcasts.

"I never set out to build the biggest podcast. I set out to have the conversations I wished I could overhear." — Steven Bartlett

What Is The Diary of a CEO?

The Diary of a CEO (often abbreviated DOAC) is a long-form interview podcast where Steven Bartlett sits down with world-leading experts, entrepreneurs, scientists, athletes, and cultural icons for deep, unfiltered conversations. Episodes typically run about 1.5 hours and cover business, psychology, health, relationships, neuroscience, and personal development.

Unlike many podcasts that stay surface-level, DOAC is known for going deep. Steven's interviewing style combines genuine curiosity with emotional vulnerability — he's not afraid to share his own struggles, push back on guests' ideas, or sit in uncomfortable silences. Many guests have said their DOAC interview was the most honest conversation they've ever had on a podcast.

What Makes DOAC Different

  • No topic is off-limits — from sex and trauma to billion-dollar business strategy
  • Long-form depth — guests have space to fully develop their ideas
  • Emotional honesty — Steven creates a space where guests open up in ways they rarely do elsewhere

Podcast Format & Style

Each episode follows a consistent but flexible structure:

Typical Episode Structure

  • Cold open (0–2 min) — A powerful clip from the episode to hook viewers
  • Steven's intro (2–5 min) — Brief context on the guest and why they matter
  • The conversation (5 min – 1.5hr) — Deep, flowing dialogue that often takes unexpected turns
  • The last guest question — A DOAC tradition where the previous guest leaves a question for the next one (the guest doesn't know it's coming)
  • Sponsor reads — Integrated naturally throughout, often personalized by Steven

The show records in Steven's signature studio — a minimalist, dimly-lit space with two chairs facing each other. The intimate setting is intentional: it strips away distractions and encourages honesty.

Top 10 Lessons Across All 400+ Episodes

After analyzing hundreds of DOAC episodes, these are the recurring themes and lessons that appear again and again from the world's top performers:

1. Your Habits Define Your Identity

From James Clear to Andrew Huberman to Steven himself — the message is consistent: you don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems. Small daily actions compound into massive results. Every guest who achieved extraordinary things points to boring consistency as the differentiator.

2. Emotional Health Is the Foundation

Whether it's Dr. Julie Smith on anxiety, Jordan Peterson on meaning, or Steven's own openness about depression — DOAC consistently emphasizes that emotional health isn't optional, it's foundational. You can't build a great business or relationship on an unstable psychological base.

3. Sleep Is Non-Negotiable

Matthew Walker's episode was a watershed moment. Combined with Huberman's protocols and Chatterjee's clinical experience, the message is clear: sleep is the single most impactful health intervention available — and most people are chronically sleep-deprived.

4. Failure Is Data, Not Destiny

Every entrepreneur on the show — Hormozi, Bartlett, Blakely, Vaynerchuk — has a failure story they credit for their eventual success. The reframe: failure isn't the opposite of success, it's the price of admission.

5. Relationships Are a Skill

From attachment theory to communication frameworks, DOAC's relationship episodes teach that love isn't just a feeling — it's a learnable skill set. Understanding your attachment style, love language, and communication patterns can transform your relationships.

6. Environment Beats Willpower

Clear, Huberman, and Hormozi all agree: design your environment for success rather than relying on discipline. Remove friction from good habits, add friction to bad ones. Your willpower is finite; your environment is controllable.

7. Health Is Wealth (Literally)

Gut health, nutrition, exercise, and stress management aren't just "nice to have" — they directly impact cognitive performance, decision-making, and earning potential. Investing in your body is the highest-ROI investment you can make.

8. Question Everything You Were Taught

A recurring meta-lesson: most of our beliefs about success, money, relationships, and happiness were inherited, not chosen. First-principles thinking — questioning assumptions and reasoning from fundamentals — separates high performers from everyone else.

9. Vulnerability Is Strength

Steven models this himself — crying on camera, sharing business failures, admitting uncertainty. His guests follow suit. The lesson: the willingness to be vulnerable creates deeper connections, better leadership, and faster personal growth.

10. Start Before You're Ready

Whether it's Mel Robbins' 5-second rule or Steven's own decision to drop out — the message is: action precedes clarity. You'll never feel 100% ready. The people who win are the ones who start anyway and figure it out along the way.

What is Steven Bartlett's podcast about?

How many episodes does Diary of a CEO have?

As of February 2026, DOAC has over 400 episodes across multiple seasons. New episodes are released multiple times per week, with both video (YouTube) and audio (Spotify, Apple Podcasts) versions available.

Is Diary of a CEO the biggest podcast in the UK?

Yes. DOAC has held the #1 spot on both Spotify and Apple Podcasts in the UK, and consistently ranks in the global top 10 across all podcast categories. The YouTube channel has over 10 million subscribers.

Where can I listen to Diary of a CEO?

DOAC is available everywhere: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube (full video episodes), Google Podcasts, Amazon Music, and all major podcast platforms. YouTube is recommended for the full experience as Steven's interviews are filmed in high quality.

What is the "last guest question" on DOAC?

It's a signature segment where the previous episode's guest leaves a question for the next guest — but the next guest doesn't know it's coming. This creates spontaneous, often profound moments as guests react to unexpected questions from strangers.

His key insight: bet on yourself and outwork everyone.
