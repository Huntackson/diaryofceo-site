🏆 Tier 1: Absolute Must-Listen (Top 5)

#1

Alex Hormozi — "The $100M Framework for 2026"

Views: 12M+ · Category: Business & Wealth

Hormozi returned to the podcast with an updated breakdown of how he evaluates businesses in 2026. The episode covers his "value equation" in detail — why most entrepreneurs are solving the wrong problems and how to identify offers that practically sell themselves. His framework for pricing, scaling, and customer acquisition is arguably the single most valuable 90 minutes of business content released this year.

"The goal isn't to make money. The goal is to become the kind of person who makes money inevitably." — Alex Hormozi
#2

Dr. Andrew Huberman — "The Neuroscience of Peak Performance"

Views: 9.5M+ · Category: Health & Science

Huberman broke down the latest neuroscience research on dopamine, focus, and daily routines. What makes this episode stand out is the practical protocol he shared — a morning routine backed by peer-reviewed studies that listeners can implement immediately. His explanation of why most productivity advice fails at a neurological level was eye-opening.

#3

Brené Brown — "Vulnerability in Leadership"

Views: 8.2M+ · Category: Leadership & Psychology

Brown's conversation with Bartlett was raw and unfiltered. She challenged the "hustle culture" narrative that dominates entrepreneurship podcasts and made a compelling case for why emotional intelligence is the most undervalued skill in business. The section on dealing with public criticism resonated deeply with creators and leaders alike.

#4

Chris Williamson — "Why Men Are Struggling & What to Do About It"

Views: 11M+ · Category: Society & Psychology

This episode went viral across every platform. Williamson and Bartlett discussed the male loneliness epidemic, dating dynamics in 2025-2026, and why traditional advice is failing young men. The conversation was nuanced, avoiding both extremes, and offered genuine solutions backed by data. It remains one of the most-shared podcast episodes of the year.

#5

Dr. Rangan Chatterjee — "The 4 Pillars of Health (Updated for 2026)"

Views: 7.8M+ · Category: Health & Wellness

Chatterjee returned with updated research on sleep, nutrition, movement, and stress. His "micro-habit" approach — making health changes so small they're impossible to fail — has helped millions of listeners transform their daily routines without extreme diets or workout plans.

🔥 Tier 2: Exceptional Episodes (6-15)

#6

Simon Sinek — "How to Find Work You Love in the AI Age"

Sinek reframed the AI anxiety conversation. Instead of asking "will AI take my job?", he explored how to find meaningful work when automation handles the routine. His concept of "infinite games" applied to career planning was a standout moment. Read the full summary →

#7

Gary Vaynerchuk — "Content, Commerce & the Creator Economy in 2026"

Gary Vee's energy was matched by genuine depth in this episode. He laid out why most creators plateau and his framework for building businesses — not just audiences — from content. His prediction about the "unbundling of universities" through creator-led education was prescient.

#8

Dr. Mindy Pelz — "Fasting, Hormones & Women's Health"

A crucial episode for female listeners. Pelz explained why popular fasting protocols can actually harm women's hormonal health and offered cycle-based alternatives. The distinction between male and female metabolic responses to fasting was information most people had never heard.

#9

Robert Greene — "The Laws of Human Nature Applied to Modern Business"

Greene's mastery of historical examples made this episode feel like a masterclass. He connected patterns from historical figures to modern startup culture, explaining why certain founders succeed and others self-destruct. His section on "the law of grandiosity" was a wake-up call for ambitious entrepreneurs.

#10

Mel Robbins — "The Let Them Theory in Practice"

Robbins shared practical frameworks for boundary-setting and decision-making. Her "Let Them" theory — the idea that you should stop trying to control others' behaviour — became a cultural moment on social media after this episode aired.

#11

Tim Ferriss — "Deconstructing Success in 2026"

Two of the world's best interviewers in conversation together. Ferriss shared his updated morning routine, his current investment thesis, and why he's shifted from "4-hour" thinking to long-term compounding in every area of life.

#12

Dr. Matthew Walker — "Sleep Masterclass: What's Changed"

Walker returned with updated research demolishing common sleep myths. His revelation about the relationship between sleep and Alzheimer's risk was sobering. Practical tips included the exact temperature, timing, and light exposure protocols for optimal sleep.

#13-15

Jay Shetty, Dr. Paul Conti, Codie Sanchez

Three powerful episodes covering purpose, mental health, and "boring businesses" respectively. Shetty's framework for finding purpose without a quarter-life crisis, Conti's explanation of trauma responses, and Sanchez's contrarian investment thesis all earned spots in the top 15. Key Themes Across 2025-2026 Episodes

Looking at the best episodes from this period, several patterns emerge:

1. The AI Conversation Has Matured

Early AI episodes were about fear and hype. The 2025-2026 episodes focus on practical adaptation — how to use AI tools, which skills become more valuable, and how to future-proof careers without panic.

2. Men's Mental Health Takes Centre Stage

Multiple top episodes addressed the male loneliness epidemic, purpose crisis, and relationship struggles. Bartlett has created space for these conversations without falling into toxic extremes on either side.

3. Health Content Gets More Nuanced

The best health episodes moved beyond generic "eat well, sleep more" advice. Gender-specific protocols, microbiome science, and evidence-based supplement recommendations show the podcast maturing alongside its audience.

4. Business Advice Becomes More Accessible

Guests like Hormozi and Sanchez are democratising business knowledge. The emphasis has shifted from "hustle harder" to "think smarter" — frameworks anyone can apply regardless of starting capital. For a deep dive, check our business advice summaries.
